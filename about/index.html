01 — The story

Ministry, cybersecurity, and AI may look like separate worlds. They are not.

I spent years teaching, leading, and listening to people in ministry. Today, much of my work and learning happens in cybersecurity and AI. The tools changed, the questions got more technical, but the responsibility stayed the same: tell the truth, protect people, and pay attention to who may be carrying more than we can see.

That is why a Bible reflection can sit next to a security project here. What are we building? Who does it help? Who could it hurt? What would it look like to do the next part faithfully? Those are not separate questions for me.

Technology does not need a layer of spiritual language painted over it. It needs people willing to remember that every technical decision eventually touches somebody.
02 — The posture

People first does not mean truth second.

I want the writing here to be warm enough that people feel welcome and direct enough that the hard question does not disappear. If somebody is afraid, confused, or carrying more than we can see, I want to name that before telling them what to do. Then we can ask what is true, who is affected, and what love requires from us.

  1. Start with what is actually happening.
  2. Ask who is affected and what they may be carrying.
  3. Bring Scripture and the way of Jesus into the real question, not around it.
  4. Be honest about what I know, what I do not know, and what still needs work.
  5. End with something useful we can practice, build, or do next.
