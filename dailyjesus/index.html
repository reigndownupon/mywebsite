Peacemakers

Day 1 · July 26, 2026
“Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called sons of God.” Matthew 5:9, BSB

Description: Jesus blesses people who do more than want peace. They step into tension and help make peace possible.

Reflection: Jesus does not ask us to pretend conflict is not there. He calls us to step toward it differently. Peacemaking may mean listening before defending ourselves, telling the truth without trying to wound, or taking the first step toward repair when we cannot control the other person’s response. Peace is not weakness. It is choosing to reflect God in the middle of tension.

What this brings up for me

There is always a struggle in desiring more money and thinking it will fix the problems. I tell myself that if I just had a little more, we would be okay. But this tension creates stress and strife. Instead of focusing on the here and now, I stay focused on the need for more. I wish this tension would fix itself, but it does not.

This is hard for me because many of us have been taught to keep the peace—to appease others or become people pleasers. But that is not what we are called to do. We are called to be peacemakers. It is difficult to imagine what making peace looks like instead of simply keeping it, especially when we do not control most of the variables in our lives.

Surrender is the first word that comes to mind. I need to surrender to the fact that this is what God has called me to. I am not responsible for controlling the outcome. I am responsible for obedience.

Takeaways

  • The constant desire for more can pull me away from the life God has placed in front of me.
  • Keeping the peace through people-pleasing is not the same as making peace.
  • Peacemaking requires truth, courage, and surrender.
  • God has called me to obedience, not to control the outcome.
