Being Confident of This

“And I am sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ.” Philippians 1:6 (ESV)

I need to believe this every day in order to move on. I have sinned in ministry; I have sinned out of ministry. I have shown mercy and grace; I have held grudges and hurt people on purpose. All while claiming to follow Jesus. When I gave my life to Christ, a counselor preached this verse over my life. It is both encouraging and overwhelming. I look at people like Paul, and although he names his own sin and weakness, I still struggle to see it. Fortunately, Peter’s failures are a little easier to see, as we see him called out by Paul and yet also see him commissioned by Jesus. My prayer is to continue in this blessing, trusting that God will continue to work on me until I am complete. This means that I am not complete yet, but God has not given up on me.

How can I live into this the way Christ asks me to? How can I be real and incomplete, letting my sins be known and seen so that they cannot take root within me? How do I live into the dichotomy that, while I am following Jesus, I will still do some horrible things and yet claim that Jesus is Lord? Following Jesus doesn’t make me immune to causing harm, but it requires repentance and healing so that harm does not become the norm or something expected of believers.

How will I make amends to those I have hurt, especially those whom I love the most? How do I share with my wife that I was wrong to tell her how to care for our sons, as if I somehow care more or am perfect? How do I tell the students I once led that I was sinful, didn’t have great theology, and wasn’t the perfect pastor for them? How do I tell my parents, whom I have little patience with, that Jesus has changed me when they still see a boy throwing a tantrum when they ask questions or need me to be a gracious listener?

When I am complete, or when I have moments of truly living into the call Jesus has for me, how do I share that as Christ and not as my own goodness or glory? How do I let my new creation in Christ be seen instead of the old me and my old ways, even when they sneak out of the new me because my desire to sin outweighs my desire to follow? Here I am. Here is the true me, depravity and all. I am a sinner who has been saved and lacks completion. I will be complete one day in Christ, and until then, I will fight my sinful will by trying to love Christ more and follow Him better.

This has been magnified by having sons. It would be so nice to be a girl dad, knowing my daughter would one day look for the qualities in a man that are the best of me. With my sons, I know they will grow up to be like me, the best of me, but also the worst. I see this in my son each and every day. When I yell, he learns to yell. When I say things like, “Knock it off,” he starts to realize that when he doesn’t want me to do something, he can shout, “Knock it off.” When I have no patience, he starts to have no patience.

Yet somehow, I don’t do the same with Christ. A Christian is just a “little Christ,” the same way my son is a little me. I need to be more like my son in this way, looking at Christ and mimicking Him the way my son mimics me, seeing Him and learning to be more like Him each day. This is the only way my son will learn to follow Jesus through me. He will become more like me each day, and if I become more like Christ each day, so will he.

There are so many moments as a father when I feel unqualified. I feel like I wasn’t meant to do this, like I can’t be a good dad. All he needs is for me to love him and be patient and present. So I will continue to learn and grow and be confident that one day Christ will complete me. I pray my son learns that and sees it.

Thank You, Jesus, for not giving up on me. Thank You for letting us know up front that it’s going to be hard, but that You won’t give up until we are complete in You. Amen.
